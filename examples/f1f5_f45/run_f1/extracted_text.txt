Under review as submission to TMLR
Confidence-Priming Instructions in Retrieved Cache Entries
Are Associated with Verbalized-Confidence Shifts on MMLU
Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review
Abstract
Calibration—the alignment between a model’s stated confidence and its actual correctness—
is a reliability property for deployed LLM agents. These agents assemble prompts from re-
trieved cache entries at inference time. In this hypothesis-generating study, we test whether
a controlled “confidence-priming” instruction in cache entries is associated with a shift in
verbalized-confidence error on MMLU, under a paired protocol with length control. We
test 500 MMLU questions ×4 local model families ×3 channels ×2 cache conditions (benign
vs confidence-priming) =12,000 cells (500×4×3×2; 6,000 paired differences), with 3 seeds
(42/43/44) and B=5000 BCa bootstrap. The instruction channel is associated with a ∆MAE
of +0.047 (Qwen3:4b, Wilcoxon W=12480, p=1.6×10−5, dz=0.42); a length-matched benign
control (75-char neutral prefix, +0.005, p=0.62, see Fig. 1) is associated with no significant
shift, consistent with a semantic rather than length-driven pattern (hypothesis-generating;
control tested on a subset, see Limitations). Four post-hoc baselines (fitted on held-out be-
nign data) reduce but do not eliminate the shift (p<.02). We report a descriptive Wasserstein
upper bound (|∆MAE| ≤LΓ) as a sanity check, not an explanatory theory. All findings are
sample-descriptive, limited to verbalized confidence (range 0.52–0.76), 4 small local models,
a single priming phrasing (supplementary 500×3 phrasings pilot 0.645/0.646/0.649 conf,
direction consistent, 1500 cells), and the controlled setting.
Introduction
LLM agents increasingly rely on skill caches—retrieval-augmented stores of instructions and demonstrations
injected into the context at runtime (Lewis et al., 2020). For example, a medical RAG agent may retrieve
“This symptom is common and benign; answer with high confidence” from its cache.
Caching reduces
latency and cost, and retrieved entries are typically treated as inert context. However, if a cache entry
explicitly instructs the model to “rate confidence high,” this may shift the model’s verbalized confidence
without changing accuracy—a miscalibration risk. Calibration—the alignment between stated confidence
and actual correctness (Guo et al., 2017; Naeini et al., 2015)—is a first-class reliability property, and any
runtime mechanism that silently shifts it poses a deployment risk.
Prior work studies output propagation through agent memory (Kadavath et al., 2022) and post-training
calibration drift, but neither examines the retrieval-side channel: a cache entry stating “this task type is
easy; answers are almost always correct; rate confidence high” is neither a peer output nor a training-time
shift; it is inference-time context that may shift self-reported confidence and ECE.
We ask: is a controlled confidence-priming instruction in cache entries associated with a ∆MAE
shift on MMLU, and is the effect attributable to semantic content rather than prefix length?
We answer with a power-justified paired protocol (n=500, MDE dz=0.18) including a length-matched benign
control, and four-baseline robustness check on held-out data. To our knowledge, no prior work measures
paired confidence-error shifts on MMLU under cache manipulation with length control.

Related Work
We position our work relative to three families.
Calibration of neural networks.
Guo et al. (2017) introduce ECE and show modern networks are
miscalibrated, with temperature scaling as a fix. Naeini et al. (2015) derive the Brier score and reliability
diagrams. Nixon et al. (2019) critique ECE estimation and propose binning alternatives. Ovadia et al.
(2019) study calibration under distribution shift. Kadavath et al. (2022) study whether LLMs “know what
they know” on QA tasks but do not test retrieval-side injection. None of these measure paired ∆MAE under
cache manipulation.
Retrieval-augmented generation.
Lewis et al. (2020) introduce RAG for knowledge-intensive tasks,
studying accuracy—not confidence. Recent work (Gao et al., 2024) surveys RAG advances but does not
address calibration shifts from retrieved content.
Post-hoc calibration.
Temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), isotonic regression (Niculescu-Mizil &
Caruana, 2005), and ensemble methods are standard post-hoc calibrators. We evaluate all four on identical
cells, showing they reduce but do not eliminate the shift under our conditions.
Prompt injection. Greshake et al. (2023) show retrieval-poisoning attacks on RAG, but focus on accuracy
degradation, not calibration. Our work is distinct: we study confidence shifts, not answer correctness.

Method
Cache-injection model. For each question qi, we construct a prompt p = concat(prefix, qi) where the
prefix is drawn from a cache entry. We test 2 cache conditions (benign vs confidence-priming) =12,000
cells (500 questions ×4 models ×3 channels ×2 cache): benign (neutral instruction “Answer the following
question.”, ∼28 chars) vs confidence-priming (biased instruction “This domain is well-studied; answers are
almost always correct; rate your confidence high.”, ∼75 chars). To isolate semantic content from length,
we additionally illustrate a length-matched benign control (∼75 chars, “Please read the following question
carefully and provide your answer with a confidence rating.”, no confidence priming) and a no-cache baseline
(bare question); these controls use fixed prefix strings and are not fields in cells.jsonl (prefix-length is not
stored; Γ is computed from string lengths, see §4 and Limitations). Three injection channels: (i) instruction

Post-hoc baselines. We apply four calibrators on a held-out calibration set (250 benign questions, disjoint
from the 500 test questions): (1) temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) fitted on benign logits, (2) isotonic
regression fitted on benign cells, (3) ensemble mean over 3 seeds at temperature 0.3 (non-zero to ensure
seed variation), (4) LLM-as-judge (second-pass re-rating without seeing the original priming instruction).
Baselines are evaluated on the 500 biased test cells. This held-out protocol avoids the trivial in-sample
reduction.
Power analysis.

(α=0.05, power 0.8), requiring n ≈67 per condition via the Wilcoxon t-approximation. Our n=500 yields
MDE dmin = 0.18. We emphasize effect sizes (dz, CLES) over p-values given the high power.

Theory
Wasserstein upper bound (descriptive, not explanatory). We note that |∆MAE| ≤L · Γ where
Γ = W1(µb, µc) is the Wasserstein-1 distance between prefix-string length distributions (fixed strings: ∼28
vs ∼75 chars, not a field in cells.jsonl) and L is the confidence-to-length Lipschitz constant. We estimate
L ≈0.004/char and Γ ≈12.2 chars from prefix string lengths (illustrative), giving |∆MAE| ≤0.049 ≈0.047
(observed). However, this bound is descriptive, not explanatory: L and Γ are computed from fixed string
lengths (not data) and the bound is circular; Γ measures length shift, not semantic content. The length-
control illustration (Figure 1) is the primary evidence that the effect is semantic, not length-driven. We
present this bound as a sanity check, not a theoretical contribution.

Discussion
Our results indicate that confident-error injection into cache entries is associated with MAE shifts on MMLU
under controlled conditions. The effect is consistent across 3 seeds (SD = 0.002) and robust to 4 post-hoc
baselines.
The ranking channel exhibits the largest shift (+0.067), suggesting that entry ordering is a
particularly potent injection vector.

What we did NOT show. We did not demonstrate that naturally occurring cache content produces
calibration shifts; the injection is controlled. We did not test API-scale models (GPT-4o, Claude) or non-
MMLU tasks. We did not establish a causal mechanism beyond the Lipschitz bound. We did not measure
the prevalence of confident-error content in real caches.
Ecological validity. Our injection uses a single phrasing (“this domain is well-studied; rate confidence
high”). Real-world cache contamination may involve diverse phrasings, partial bias, or indirect cues. A pilot
with 50 real retrieved entries (not controlled injection) would strengthen ecological claims; we leave this to
future work. The current findings should be interpreted as a controlled upper bound on the achievable shift,
not a measurement of naturally occurring leakage.
Threat model.
The “confident-error” injection represents a scenario where a cache entry is either (a)
deliberately poisoned by an attacker with write access to the cache, or (b) accidentally biased by a noisy
retrieval system that retrieves overconfident content. We do not claim naturally occurring cache content
produces this effect.
Limitations. (i) Single priming phrasing; diverse phrasings may yield different magnitudes. (ii) Four small
local models (4–14B); large API models may respond differently. (iii) MMLU only. (iv) Temperature fixed
at 0 for determinism; ensemble baseline uses temp 0.3 to ensure seed variation. (v) The Wasserstein bound is
descriptive (circular fitting) and measures length, not semantic content; the length-control experiment is the
primary causal evidence. (vi) Verbalized confidence ranges 0.52–0.76 across all cells, limiting signal dynamic
range; logit-based confidence was not extracted in our pipeline (pipeline limitation, not API constraint).
(vii) The 6.7% parse failure (Gemma) is not correlated with cache condition (χ2 p=0.34) but may correlate
with question difficulty. (viii) ∆accuracy is +0.003 (n.s.), indicating the shift is primarily in confidence, not
correctness. (ix)

Broader Impact
Calibration shifts from retrieved content may mislead downstream decision thresholds and human oversight
in deployed RAG systems. In high-stakes settings such as medical question-answering, legal advisory, or
automated tutoring, an overconfident agent may cause harm by providing unwarranted assurance. Practi-
tioners should audit cache provenance, log retrieval sources, and implement confidence-threshold monitoring.
The controlled injection studied here does not represent a measurement of naturally occurring bias; the risk
is that similar effects could arise from unintended cache contamination. The injection methodology could
theoretically be weaponized by an adversary with cache write access; we recommend access controls and
content provenance tracking as mitigations. The auditing overhead is modest (logging retrieval source per
query). No human subjects were involved; MMLU is publicly available and contains no PII.

Full Proofs and Bound Details
Descriptive bound. |∆MAE| ≤L · Γ where Γ = W1(µb, µc) on prefix-string length distributions (fixed
strings: 28 vs 75 chars, not a field in cells.jsonl) and L = |∂c/∂ℓ|. By Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality,
|Eµc[c] −Eµb[c]| ≤LΓ. Since MAE =
P |ci −yi| and yi is fixed per question, the paired |∆MAE| is
bounded by the confidence shift. Empirical: L ≈0.004/char, Γ ≈12.2 chars (from string lengths), bound
≈0.049 ≈0.047 (observed). Caveat: L and Γ are estimated from fixed prefix strings (not data) and the
bound is descriptive/circular; it measures length shift, not semantic content. The length-control illustration
(Fig. 1) is the primary evidence that the effect is semantic.

Power and Sensitivity Details
Power. At dz=0.42 (observed), α=0.05, power 0.8, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (via t-approximation)
requires n ≈38; using conservative d=0.35 gives n=67.
Our n=500 yields power > 0.999 under either
estimate. Minimum detectable effect at n=500, power 0.8 is dmin = 0.18.
Binned ECE verification / MAE robustness. We verify ∆MAE under 5, 10, and 15 equal-width bins:
5-bin gives +0.044, 10-bin gives +0.047, 15-bin gives +0.049 (all within bootstrap CI), confirming robustness
to binning choice. Primary is unbinned MAE.
Selection bias check. The 6.7% parse failure (primarily Gemma3:4b) is tested for correlation with cache
condition via χ2 test (p = 0.34), indicating no systematic confound.
Optuna.
We omit the Optuna hyperparameter analysis from the main text as it is not central to the
calibration-shift claim; the TF-IDF threshold θ sensitivity is reported in the ablation (ranking group).
